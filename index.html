🎯 Making Practice Actually Count

Practice only helps when you can see it working. Keep sessions short and repeatable: five to ten minutes per drill, the same drill, several times a week. The toolkit records each run so you are comparing like with like.

Use the recoil practice mode to learn a weapon's pattern slowly, then speed up. When a drill stops improving, change one thing — your sensitivity note, your loadout, or the range of the target — and log the result. Over a couple of weeks the trend line will tell you more than any single session ever could.

👥 Planning Your Squad

Squad planning works best when everyone knows their job before the match begins. Add each player once, then build plans on top of that roster. Name each plan after the map or mode it is for, so nobody has to guess which plan to load.

If a plan keeps failing, edit it rather than starting fresh. Your saved plans form a small history of what your group has tried, and that history is genuinely useful when you review a session.

📊 Reading Your Match Analytics

The analytics view is built from the matches you log. It highlights which loadouts you perform best with, which ones you keep losing with, and how your accuracy notes trend over time. Log honestly — a padded entry only fools your own charts.

A practical habit: after every session, look at the summary for sixty seconds and write one sentence about what you would change next time. The toolkit keeps that note attached to the session, so future you can see the reasoning.

💡 Helpful Tips

  • Name loadouts by purpose, not by date — "Close Range" is easier to find than "Build 7".
  • Keep one profile as a scratch pad for experiments so your main profiles stay clean.
  • Log matches the same day. Notes written later are always vaguer.
  • Back up your toolkit folder occasionally if you have built up a lot of match history.
  • Review your analytics weekly rather than after every single match.
