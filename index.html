About

Maya Keshk is an economist specialising in macroeconomic analysis, energy markets, global trade and geopolitical risk. Her profile sits at the intersection of economic and business analysis: she assesses how macroeconomic conditions, policy decisions and geopolitical developments shape markets, and translates that assessment into implications for investment, strategy and commercial decisions.

This combined perspective allows her to move between the national and the company level, from interest rate cycles, trade flows and energy supply routes to market sizing, competitive positioning and feasibility. Her focus is on identifying emerging risks and opportunities before they are widely recognised, and on building the evidence required to act on them, with particular attention to the Gulf, Egypt and the wider Middle East.

Her approach combines financial and economic modelling, econometrics and index design with political and policy analysis. She holds a B.Sc. in Economics from London South Bank University, is completing an M.A. in International Relations at Liverpool John Moores University, and works fluently in both Arabic and English.

Published paper · European Governance Lab · 27 October 2026

Beyond Energy Independence: Maritime Security as the Missing Pillar of European Energy Security

Question
After 2022, did Europe’s move away from Russian pipeline gas shift its energy risk to the sea?
Approach
Quantitative indicators of gas imports and LNG flows, with case studies of the Red Sea disruption, the 2021 Suez blockage and the 2026 Hormuz closure.
Finding
Diversification cut dependence on one supplier but raised exposure to maritime chokepoints. The paper recommends crisis coordination, more LNG storage and terminal capacity, and diversified shipping routes.

Egypt’s consumer credit boom, 2021 to 2025

An index built by Maya Keshk at the Ministry of Planning and Economic Development from public Financial Regulatory Authority data, tracking how many people use consumer finance and how much they borrow each month.

Key findings
Credit is broadening, fastUsers grew about 180% in a year while financing grew about 39%, so the average amount per user roughly halved to around EGP 8,500. More people are borrowing smaller amounts.
Rate cuts lit the second waveFrom the first rate cut in April 2025 to August, users rose about 83% and financing nearly doubled, consistent with the inverse link between rates and borrowing.
Durables lead, but risk is buildingCars, electronics and appliances take most of the value. With weak real incomes and little collateral, the pace of growth is worth close monitoring for repayment risk.

Independent project · Market research

Lindo: quantitative consumer research and growth strategy

Question
How can a small Egyptian online retailer compete with Amazon and Jumia?
Approach
Designed and analysed a survey of 166 online shoppers aged 25 to 50 on brand health, satisfaction, drivers and barriers.
Finding
Lindo leads on customer experience, with a net promoter score of 96. Authenticity (71%) and website usability decide purchases, which shaped a five step growth roadmap.
